A poverty number is the answer to four decisions; change one decision and the number changes, so a good diagnosis states its decisions and uses more than one number.

      All the numbers, and what they mean

      Lorenz curve

      Line up everyone from poorest to richest and add up their incomes. If everyone had the same, the curve would be the diagonal. The Gini is the gap between the diagonal and the curve: the shaded area, as a share of the whole triangle under the diagonal.

      Why "per equivalent adult"?

      A family of five does not need five times what a single person needs: they share a kitchen and a fridge. An equivalence scale divides household income by a number of "equivalent adults".

      The example is the Wójcik family from Session 3: PLN 2,715 for two adults and three children. Per equivalent adult they have about PLN 1,131, below the line for a single person, although the family total looks far above it. Different scales move different families above or below the line, which is why reports often check both.

      The households

      Type new incomes, change who lives in each household, add or remove households. "Before" is the starting income; "after" adds the economy change and the benefit. Household size matters only when you count people.

      Try this

      1. Make household 3 (the pensioner living alone) poor without changing her income. Which line can do that, and which cannot?
      2. Find the smallest benefit to households with children that brings the anchored poverty rate to zero. Then compare its cost with the poverty gap before the change.
      3. Raise only the richest household's income by 100%. What happens to the poverty rate, the gap, S80/S20 and the Gini? Is this society now "worse"?
      4. Switch from households to people. Why does the poverty rate go up, although no income changed?
      5. Run the recession. Which line tells you that people got poorer? Which one hides it?
      6. Make a benefit means-tested, then universal, with the same amount. Which one costs less, and which one cuts the gap more per unit spent?

      Words used here

      MedianSort everyone from poorest to richest; the median is the income of the person in the middle. With an even number, take the average of the two middle values: for 10, 14, 18, 22, 26, 30, 36, 44, 60, 140 it is (26 + 30) ÷ 2 = 28. One very rich household does not move it.
      MeanTotal income divided by the number of people (or households). One very rich household pulls it up.
      Relative lineA share (usually 60%) of this year's median. Poverty means falling behind the normal standard of living. It moves when the median moves. This is the EU "at risk of poverty" line.
      Anchored lineThe relative line of a starting year, kept fixed (only adjusted for prices). It shows whether the poor are better off than they were.
      Absolute lineThe cost of a basket of basic needs, adjusted only for prices. Poland's extreme poverty line (the subsistence minimum) and the World Bank's $3.00 and $8.30 a day lines (2021 prices, PPP) are absolute.
      Poverty rate (headcount)The share below the line. Statistics offices count people, not households. It does not care whether someone misses the line by 1 or by 10.
      Poverty gapFor each poor person, the shortfall: line minus income. Add up the shortfalls. That is the amount a perfectly targeted transfer would need to end poverty. The gap index divides it by (line × everyone).
      S80/S20Income of the richest 20% divided by the income of the poorest 20%.
      Palma ratioIncome of the richest 10% divided by the income of the poorest 40%.
      Gini coefficient0 if everyone has the same income, 1 if one person has everything. Often written ×100 (Poland about 28–30). It sees the whole distribution, but the same Gini can hide very different societies.
